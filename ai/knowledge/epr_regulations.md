# Vietnam EPR Regulations — Summary for RENOVA

## What is EPR?
Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) is a mandatory legal framework in Vietnam under the 2020 Law on Environmental Protection. It requires producers and importers of specific products — including plastic packaging — to take financial and operational responsibility for recycling and end-of-life treatment.

## Key Regulations
- **Decree 08/2022/ND-CP**: Established implementation rules, product categories, and the recycling roadmap
- **Decree 05/2025/ND-CP**: Updated mandatory recycling rates and specifications for plastic products and packaging
- **Decree 110/2026/ND-CP**: Effective May 25, 2026, consolidates previous EPR provisions into a standalone regulatory instrument

## Implementation Timeline
- **January 1, 2024**: Comprehensive EPR implementation began for packaging (including plastic)
- **January 1, 2025**: Expanded to include electrical and electronic equipment
- **2027**: Planned expansion to include vehicles

## Compliance Options
Producers and importers must either:
1. **Organize recycling** at the mandatory rate prescribed by law, OR
2. **Pay a financial contribution** to the Vietnam Environmental Protection Fund (VEPF) based on a statutory cost formula

## EPR Fee Structure
- Standard EPR fee rate: approximately 15,000 VND per kg of packaging released to market
- Companies can achieve up to 40% reduction in EPR obligations by demonstrating recycling partnerships

## Vietnam Plastic Waste Context
- Vietnam generated approximately 3.8 million tonnes of plastic waste in 2023 (~38.7 kg per capita)
- Vietnam ranked 7th globally in mismanaged plastic waste volume (2.1 million tonnes as of 2025)
- Recycling industry is scaling up, with leading firms tripling production to 49,000+ metric tonnes of recycled plastic annually by 2025

## How RENOVA Helps with EPR Compliance
RENOVA provides a direct pathway for companies to offset their EPR obligations:
1. Companies with MLP packaging waste can partner with RENOVA
2. RENOVA converts their waste into breeze blocks (each brick uses 1.05 kg of plastic waste)
3. The partnership counts toward the company's recycling obligations under EPR
4. Companies can also purchase RENOVA bricks for office/factory landscaping, with costs partially offset by EPR fund credits
5. This creates a circular economy loop: waste becomes building material, companies save on EPR fees

## Key Challenges in Vietnam
- Ineffective waste separation at source remains a primary bottleneck
- Need for robust monitoring and reporting systems
- Upcoming bans on non-degradable plastic bags (2026)
- Push toward eco-design and circular economy practices
